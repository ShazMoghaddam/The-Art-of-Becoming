# BUILDING RESILIENCE IN THE FACE OF CHANGE

## A personal essay on continuing forward when life doesn't go to plan

### Gathered by Shaz Moghaddam

---

Change has a strange way of arriving without asking.

Sometimes it enters quietly through small shifts in routine. Other times it crashes in through loss, uncertainty, failure, or an ending you weren't prepared for.

No matter how carefully you plan, life eventually reminds you that control is more limited than you'd hoped.

I remember one particular period where several things shifted at once — work, relationships, a sense of direction I thought I had. Nothing catastrophic by anyone else's measure. But I remember sitting at my kitchen table one morning, coffee going cold, looking out the window and genuinely not knowing what I was supposed to do next. Not in a dramatic way. Just quietly lost, in the middle of an ordinary Tuesday.

What I found in that stillness — slowly and not always gracefully — was resilience. Not the version that looks composed from the outside. The quieter kind that just keeps going.

---

### I. WHAT RESILIENCE ACTUALLY MEANS

Resilience is often misunderstood.

Many people think it means being emotionally strong all the time. Never struggling. Never admitting that something hurt. That version of resilience has never felt real to me.

What I've come to understand is simpler: resilience is about continuing forward, even when things do hurt.

Some of the steadiest people I've known are not the loudest or most confident. They are the ones who learned how to carry pain without letting it harden them. Who got knocked down, took the time they needed, and found a way to begin again.

> "Circumstances do not make the man, they only reveal him to himself."
> — Epictetus

Difficult moments reveal things about us that quieter times never would. Not just our weaknesses — but our patience, our adaptability, our capacity to rebuild. I didn't know some of those things about myself until I had no choice but to find them.

Human beings recover more than we give ourselves credit for.

We rebuild after failure, sometimes starting over with something so small it barely feels like progress. We adjust after loss in ways we couldn't have predicted beforehand. We begin again, even when part of us doesn't quite believe we can — not perfectly, not all at once, but gradually, the way most real things happen.

---

### II. WALKING THROUGH UNCERTAIN SEASONS

One of the hardest parts of change is not knowing what comes next.

We want answers. We want guarantees. We want to know that the difficult season has a clear end and something good waiting on the other side.

But life rarely offers that kind of clarity.

There have been long stretches in my own life where I genuinely didn't know how something would turn out — whether a decision was right, whether I would find steadier ground again. In those periods, I kept waiting to feel ready before moving forward, as if clarity was something that would arrive on its own if I just held on a little longer. Some days that meant going through the motions. Showing up to things without really being present. Doing the next small task just to feel like I was moving, even when I had no idea where I was going. It wasn't pretty and it wasn't smooth, but looking back, that was the resilience — not the moments of confidence, but the ordinary days where I just kept going anyway.

I know now that resilience often just means learning to walk through the uncertainty without having the answers first.

> "Pain is inevitable. Suffering is optional."
> — Haruki Murakami

That line used to frustrate me. It sounded too clean, too simple. But I've come to understand what it's pointing at. The pain of difficult seasons is often real and unavoidable. What we do have some say over is whether we add to it — through resistance, through self-blame, through refusing to let anything move.

We can allow difficult moments to define us entirely.
Or we can let them deepen us.

There is a real difference between the two.

---

### III. THE SMALL AND ORDINARY ACTS

Not every act of resilience looks dramatic.

Sometimes it looks like getting out of bed when motivation is nowhere to be found. Sometimes it looks like starting something over after it failed. Sometimes it looks like asking for help — which is harder than it sounds.

And sometimes, it just looks like resting.

We live in a culture that prizes constant movement. But resilience is not endless exhaustion. Even nature rests. Seasons change. Trees lose their leaves. The world slows down and starts again.

We are no different.

> "If one is estranged from oneself, then one is estranged from life."
> — Anne Morrow Lindbergh

During the hardest periods of change in my own life, I noticed how easy it was to lose the thread back to myself. Trapped in stress or survival mode, the simple things quietly fell away — sleep, honest conversation, time outside, moments of silence. Resilience grows stronger when you reconnect with those small things. Not through dramatic breakthroughs. Through ordinary habits, returned to steadily, day after day.

Small things done consistently matter more than we realise.

---

### IV. WHAT CHANGE TEACHES US

One thing life keeps teaching me is that change is unavoidable.

Nothing stays exactly the same — relationships evolve, careers shift, people we love grow in different directions, and sometimes we grow too, in ways that change everything around us before we've even noticed it happening. And sometimes that growth pulls things apart before it puts anything back together.

At first, that can feel frightening.

But there is also something worth noticing in it.

Imagine if nothing ever changed. No healing. No growth. No second chances. No unexpected turns that led somewhere you never could have planned for. The people you became close to because of a disruption. The things you learned about yourself only because something fell away. The directions you found only after the original one closed.

Change breaks routines. But it also breaks limitations. It breaks the version of the future you had decided was the only one available.

> "When we change, the world changes."
> — Daisaku Ikeda

Resilience doesn't begin with controlling what happens around us. It begins with strengthening the way we meet it.

Resilient people are not people who avoid difficulty. They are the ones who continue despite it. Quietly, imperfectly, and without much fanfare.

Life will keep changing. Unexpected things will keep happening. But I've come to believe that most people carry far more capacity to adapt, heal, and begin again than they give themselves credit for.

Even after the hardest seasons, people still find reasons to hope.

And maybe that, more than anything, is what resilience looks like in practice.
